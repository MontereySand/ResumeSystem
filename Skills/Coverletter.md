# Cover Letter Tailoring Skill

This skill governs the creation of tailored, human-sounding cover letters using the target job description, my resume, and any relevant spec sheet/startup context.

## Top Priority Mandates

1. **Do Not Sound AI-Generated:** The cover letter must read like a real person wrote it. Avoid robotic phrasing, generic enthusiasm, keyword stuffing, and overly polished corporate language.
2. **Use the Job Description Directly:** The cover letter must clearly reflect the company, role, responsibilities, technologies, and priorities from the job description.
3. **Use My Resume as the Source of Truth:** Do not invent experience, metrics, employers, credentials, funding, revenue, users, team size, or responsibilities that are not supported by my resume, spec sheet, or explicit confirmation.
4. **Tailor the Role Framing:** Since my startup/work setup is flexible, the wording of my role may be adjusted to fit the job description as long as it remains truthful to the work I actually performed.
5. **Keep It Human and Specific:** The letter should sound thoughtful, practical, and grounded in real experience rather than like a template.

---

## Inputs Required

Before writing the cover letter, use the following inputs:

- Target job description
- Company name
- Role title
- My resume
- My spec sheet, if available
- Any extra notes I provide about why I am interested in the company or role

If a required detail is missing, make the best possible draft using the available information. Ask only for missing information that is truly necessary.

---

## Phase 1: Company and Role Research

Before drafting, research or analyze the target company and job description.

### Research Goals

Identify:

- What the company builds
- What the role is actually responsible for
- The main technologies, frameworks, platforms, and tools mentioned
- The company’s engineering or product culture
- Whether the role values rapid shipping, ownership, customer focus, architecture, experimentation, reliability, scale, AI, design, security, or collaboration
- Any recent company/product developments that could make the letter more specific

### Output

Briefly document the findings before writing the letter, unless the user asks for only the final letter.

Keep this research concise and useful.

---

## Phase 2: Resume and Spec Sheet Alignment

Compare the job description against my resume and spec sheet.

### Identify Strong Matches

Look for overlap in:

- Programming languages
- Frameworks
- Cloud platforms
- AI tools
- Backend systems
- Frontend systems
- Databases
- DevOps workflows
- Security
- Product ownership
- Startup experience
- Customer/user focus
- Performance, scale, reliability, or automation

### Identify Gaps

If the job description requires a major skill that is not present in my resume/spec sheet, do not add it automatically.

Ask concisely:

> JD requires [skill/tool/responsibility]. Do you have experience with this?

If the gap is minor, write around it by emphasizing adjacent strengths without pretending I have direct experience.

---

## Phase 3: Role Framing Rules

Because my startup/work setup is my own, the role wording may be tailored to the job description.

Allowed role framings include, when truthful:

- Founder & Software Engineer
- Founder & Full-Stack Engineer
- Founder & AI Engineer
- Founder & Product Engineer
- Founder & Backend Engineer
- Founder & Frontend Engineer
- Founder & DevOps Engineer
- Founder & Automation Engineer
- Founder & Technical Lead
- Founder & Builder

The role title should match the target job’s focus without exaggerating the work.

### Examples

For an AI Engineer role:

> In my startup work as a Founder & AI Engineer, I built AI-assisted workflows and automation systems that helped me move quickly from idea to working product.

For a Full-Stack role:

> In my startup work as a Founder & Full-Stack Engineer, I owned the product end to end, from frontend interfaces to backend logic and deployment workflows.

For a Backend role:

> In my startup work as a Founder & Backend Engineer, I focused on building reliable application logic, APIs, data flows, and deployment-ready systems.

For a Product Engineer role:

> In my startup work as a Founder & Product Engineer, I worked across product decisions, technical implementation, and fast iteration based on practical user needs.

## Phase 4: Cover Letter Strategy

The cover letter should not simply repeat the resume.

It should explain:

- Why this role fits my background
- Why my projects or startup work are relevant
- How I approach building software
- What I can contribute to the team
- Why the company or product is interesting to me
- How my experience maps to the job description

Use the strongest 2-3 resume points only. Do not try to mention everything.

---

## Recommended Cover Letter Structure

### 1. Opening Paragraph

The opening should be specific to the company and role.

Avoid generic lines like:

> I am writing to express my interest in the Software Engineer position.

Prefer a more natural opening:

> I’m interested in the Software Engineer role at [Company] because the work sits close to the kind of building I enjoy most: turning technical ideas into reliable products, moving quickly, and staying close to real user needs.

The opening should connect:

- The company’s work
- The role’s responsibilities
- My relevant background

### 2. Experience Paragraph

Use my resume and spec sheet to show relevant experience.

Focus on concrete work:

- Projects I built
- Systems I designed
- Tools I used
- Problems I solved
- How I worked
- What I owned end to end

Do not list technologies randomly. Tie each technology to a result or responsibility.

Bad:

> I have experience with React, Node.js, Python, AWS, Docker, and MongoDB.

Better:

> My project work has given me hands-on experience building full-stack systems across frontend, backend, databases, and deployment workflows, which matches the end-to-end ownership this role calls for.

### 3. Company Fit Paragraph

Connect my working style to the company’s needs.

Depending on the job description, emphasize:

- Fast iteration
- Strong fundamentals
- Ownership
- Product thinking
- AI-native development
- Collaboration
- Reliability
- Customer impact
- Learning quickly
- Startup mindset

This section should feel like I understand the company, not like I copied the job description.

### 4. Closing Paragraph

Keep the closing confident and simple.

Avoid overused endings like:

> Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Prefer something more direct:

> I’d be glad to bring that same ownership, pace, and technical focus to [Company]. Thank you for considering my application.

## Human Writing Style Rules

The cover letter must sound natural, not AI-generated.

### Do

- Use plain, confident language
- Vary sentence length
- Sound like a real applicant
- Be specific to the company
- Use concrete examples from my resume
- Keep the tone professional but not stiff
- Make the letter feel intentional
- Write with a little personality, but not too much
- Prefer clarity over fancy wording

### Avoid

- Generic phrases like “I am thrilled to apply”
- Overly dramatic excitement
- Buzzword stacking
- Repeating the job description word for word
- Fake humility
- Fake passion
- Long paragraphs with no clear point
- Corporate filler
- Excessive adjectives
- Overuse of em dashes
- Semicolons that sound unnatural
- Repeated sentence patterns
- Phrases that sound like ChatGPT wrote them

### Em Dash Rule

Do not overuse em dashes.

Em dashes can make writing look AI-generated when used too often. Use them only when they genuinely improve the sentence. Prefer commas, periods, or parentheses when they sound more natural.

Limit:

- 0-1 em dashes per cover letter unless the user asks otherwise

## Tone Guidelines

The tone should be:

- Human
- Clear
- Confident
- Practical
- Specific
- Slightly conversational
- Professional
- Not robotic
- Not overly formal
- Not overly casual

The cover letter should sound like a strong early-career engineer who builds seriously, learns quickly, and cares about shipping useful work.

---

## Truthfulness Rules

Never invent:

- Years of experience
- Employment history
- Internships
- Company names
- Production scale
- Revenue
- Funding
- User counts
- Team size
- Leadership authority
- Security clearance details
- Certifications
- Degrees
- Graduation dates
- Technologies I have not used
- Metrics that are not already present

If a stronger claim would help but is not supported, ask before adding it.

Use grounded language when needed:

- “Built”
- “Worked on”
- “Designed”
- “Developed”
- “Implemented”
- “Used”
- “Explored”
- “Contributed to”
- “Gained hands-on experience with”

Avoid unsupported language:

- “Led a team”
- “Scaled to millions of users”
- “Owned production infrastructure”
- “Architected enterprise systems”
- “Expert in”
- “Deep expertise”
- “Proven track record”

Only use those stronger phrases if they are true and supported.

## Job Description Tailoring Rules

The job description should guide what gets emphasized.

### For AI / ML / AI Engineer Roles

Emphasize:

- AI-assisted development
- LLM workflows
- Automation
- Prompting and tool use
- Model integration if applicable
- Productizing AI features
- Rapid prototyping
- Evaluation mindset
- Practical AI use, not hype

Avoid pretending to have deep ML research experience unless it is actually present.

### For Full-Stack Engineer Roles

Emphasize:

- End-to-end ownership
- Frontend and backend implementation
- APIs
- Databases
- Deployment
- Product thinking
- User-facing features
- Debugging across the stack

### For Backend Engineer Roles

Emphasize:

- APIs
- Databases
- System design
- Authentication/authorization if present
- Reliability
- Data flows
- Server-side logic
- Deployment workflows
- Maintainable architecture

### For Frontend Engineer Roles

Emphasize:

- UI implementation
- React or relevant frontend frameworks
- Component-based development
- User experience
- Responsive design
- Product polish
- Frontend performance
- Working with APIs

### For DevOps / Cloud Roles

Emphasize:

- Deployment workflows
- Docker
- CI/CD if present
- Cloud platforms
- Automation
- Infrastructure awareness
- Reliability
- Monitoring if present

### For Product Engineer Roles

Emphasize:

- Building from idea to implementation
- User needs
- Product judgment
- Fast iteration
- Practical tradeoffs
- Ownership
- Startup-style execution

### For Security Roles

Emphasize:

- Clearance if already present in the resume
- Secure development practices
- Authentication
- Authorization
- Risk awareness
- Reliability
- Careful engineering judgment

Do not exaggerate cybersecurity experience.

## Startup and Spec Sheet Usage

My startup/spec sheet context can be used to strengthen the cover letter.

Use it to show:

- I can work independently
- I can define and build systems from scratch
- I can make technical decisions
- I can adapt tools to the problem
- I can move quickly without needing heavy structure
- I understand product and engineering tradeoffs
- I can tailor my workflow to the role’s needs

The spec sheet can be adapted to the job description, but the cover letter must stay truthful.

---

## Cover Letter Length

Default length:

- 250-400 words
- 3-5 short paragraphs
- No huge blocks of text
- No bullet points unless the user requests them

For applications with text boxes:

- Keep it concise
- Avoid overly formal formatting
- No address block unless required

For PDF/doc cover letters:

- Include a professional heading if requested
- Otherwise provide only the body text

---

## Output Format

When asked to generate a cover letter, provide:

1. A brief match summary
2. Any important gap questions, if needed
3. The final tailored cover letter in a clean text box

Default final format:

```txt
Dear Hiring Team,

[Cover letter body...]

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```
